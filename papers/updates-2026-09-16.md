# Literature Update — 2026-09-16

Papers and preprints that materially affect Harnessed Agentic RL, process-level credit, scientific ideation, diversity, and the model–harness internalization/externalization boundary.

## TASPO: Reconciling Process Supervision with Outcome-Based Credit in Agentic Policy Optimization
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31077
- Date: 2026-08-31
- Core idea: privileged process supervision is used only to redistribute verified trajectory-level credit across executable actions. The outcome reward still determines update direction and average scale.
- Key distinction: fine-grained supervision is not automatically fine-grained credit; TASPO converts supervision into action-level, outcome-grounded credit.
- Why it matters: directly relevant to our branch-and-intervene idea. If Harness interventions are inserted at intermediate states, we need a principled way to assign the resulting outcome improvement to the relevant action/intervention rather than uniformly to the whole trajectory.
- Relation to our direction: strong credit-assignment prior, but it does not learn heterogeneous Harness component selection or model–Harness capability allocation.

## Theory of Agent: The Science of Internalization and Externalization for LLM-Based Agents
- URL: https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202609.0308
- Date: 2026-09-03
- Status: preprint; not peer reviewed
- Core idea: an LLM agent is a model coupled to a harness. Agent design is framed as deciding which capabilities should be internalized into model parameters and which should remain externalized in memory, tools, skills, orchestration, and other Harness components.
- Key distinction: stable/recurrent procedures may be worth internalizing; fresh information, execution, verification, persistent state, and external compute often remain valuable outside the model.
- Why it matters: very close to our current conceptual split between internalizable Harness capabilities (e.g. textual procedural skills) and persistent external capabilities (e.g. search, memory, sub-agents, branching compute).
- Relation to our direction: provides a broad theoretical framing, but does not itself solve state-conditioned Harness activation, component-level intervention credit, or dynamic allocation during Agentic RL.

## FlashREINFORCE: Critic-Free Single-Rollout Asynchronous RL for Agentic Language Models
- Project/code: https://github.com/yifanzhang-pro/FlashREINFORCE
- Date: 2026-09
- Core idea: one rollout per prompt, asynchronous collection, batch-centered REINFORCE, token importance sampling, a sequence trust region, and sample-mean optimization; no learned critic and no sibling-rollout group requirement.
- Why it matters: shows that long-horizon Agentic RL can reduce the synchronization and rollout-duplication costs of GRPO-style group sampling while maintaining stable training.
- Relation to our direction: mainly a rollout-efficiency/system baseline. It does not solve Harness intervention credit, but is relevant if our counterfactual Harness branches make rollout cost a bottleneck.

## AgentIdeaBench: Benchmarking Scientific Ideation in the Agent Era
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07611
- Date: 2026-09-07
- Core idea: evaluates scientific ideation under matched static-observation and active-exploration settings across 33 LLMs, 40 subfields, and five disciplines; originality is checked against retrieved prior art.
- Key result: active exploration improves grounding, feasibility, clarity, and specificity, but does not improve measured originality under the paper's critics. Its Scientific World Modeling loop helps mid-capability models more than frontier models, which the authors interpret as possible internalization of that reasoning pattern.
- Why it matters: directly challenges any idea-generation method that reports only final idea quality. Search/exploration may improve groundedness without increasing novelty, so originality and feasibility should be separately rewarded and measured.
- Relation to our direction: useful benchmark and diagnostic for Adaptive Harness research. It gives a natural test of whether search/world-modeling Harnesses should remain external for some models while becoming less useful as capabilities internalize.

## Creative Generation via Multi-Agent Debate: Does Debate Suppress Diversity?
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00683
- Date: 2026-09-01; EMNLP 2026 Main
- Core idea: standard multi-agent debate is convergence-driven and can suppress diversity across independent runs. Creative-MAD preserves persistent cognitive lenses and exposes agents to semantically distant peers rather than majority-like peers.
- Key result: higher lexical and semantic diversity while maintaining output quality across four creative benchmarks.
- Why it matters: directly relevant to diversity-preserving research idea generation. More critique/debate is not automatically better: an apparently stronger Harness can collapse the idea population toward one mode.
- Relation to our direction: suggests that a Harness controller should optimize not only task reward/cost but also population-level diversity, especially when deciding whether to trigger critique, debate, aggregation, or branch merging.

## SiLR: Structure-Preserving Admission and Process Reward for LLM Tool Agents
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04629
- Date: 2026-09-04
- Core idea: a runtime gate is treated as a search/admission operator. SiLR shadow-executes proposals and uses a structured product order over violation state instead of projecting progress into one scalar score; the same structured signal can be reused as a GRPO process reward.
- Key result: in its constrained control setting, the structured process reward is the only tested reward whose ungated policy exceeds the untrained base model, while scalar projections lose important state geometry.
- Why it matters: relevant to our internalization question. A process signal can improve the policy even after the runtime gate is removed, but only if the reward preserves the structure of the underlying state rather than collapsing everything into a scalar heuristic.
- Relation to our direction: supports testing whether Harness-derived supervision produces Harness-off gains. Caveat: evidence is from constrained control tasks, not general-purpose LLM agent benchmarks.

## Takeaway
The most relevant new implications are:

1. **AgentIdeaBench:** exploration and originality are separable; search/world-modeling Harnesses need component-specific evaluation rather than one aggregate idea score.
2. **Creative-MAD:** critique/debate can destroy diversity, so Harness optimization for scientific ideation needs an explicit diversity objective or constraint.
3. **SiLR:** structured process signals may transfer into the ungated policy better than scalarized heuristics, strengthening the case for Harness-off evaluation after training.
4. **TASPO + Theory of Agent:** process credit and the internalization/externalization boundary remain central to the Adaptive Harness formulation.

For our project, the remaining distinctive target is **state-conditioned selection of heterogeneous Harness actions, intervention-specific credit, diversity-aware optimization, and explicit measurement of which Harness capabilities become internalized versus remain valuable as external resources**.
