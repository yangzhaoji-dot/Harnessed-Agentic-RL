# 2026-09-04 High-Priority Literature Update

This update tracks new papers submitted on 2026-09-03 and appearing in the 2026-09-04 arXiv recent lists that materially affect process reward, credit assignment, coverage/diversity, and Agentic RL training design.

## DRACO: Fine-Grained Credit Assignment with Dynamic Rubrics for Long-Horizon Agent Training
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04094
- Date: 2026-09-03
- Category: Agentic RL; Process Reward; Credit Assignment; Dynamic Rubrics
- Core idea: dynamically generates rubrics as policy capability evolves, scores them once at trajectory end, then redistributes rubric-level judgments to the responsible steps to create differentiated per-step GRPO advantages.
- Notable point: works in an outcome-blind setting without ground-truth task-success verifiers; no trained attribution module is required.
- Reported result: +15.9 points over base on AppWorld and +5.3 over GRPO using sparse ground-truth reward; also transfers to Tau-Bench.
- Why it matters: one of the clearest new blueprints for research-idea RL when final idea quality is hard to verify programmatically. It suggests task-specific dynamic rubrics can serve as process supervision while credit is assigned only to the idea-generation/revision steps linked to each criterion.
- Priority: **Must read**.

## Spurious Advantage Hidden in GRPO
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04063
- Date: 2026-09-03
- Category: GRPO; Advantage Estimation; Agentic Search; Reward Reliability
- Core idea: identifies a failure mode where within-group normalization gives large positive advantage to rollouts that reach the correct answer by guessing rather than by useful reasoning/search.
- Proposed fix: SIGNBALANCE preserves verifier sign but replaces composition-dependent GRPO magnitude with a global scale plus stop-gradient class balancing.
- Why it matters: especially relevant if Harness adaptation or idea-graph updates use GRPO reward/advantage statistics as signals. High relative advantage is not always evidence that a branch/action is causally useful; it may be an artifact of group composition or answer-space structure.
- Priority: **Must read**.

## Rethinking On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models II: One Training Example
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04172
- Date: 2026-09-03
- Category: On-Policy Distillation; Coverage; Diversity; Post-Training
- Core idea: a single training query can recover most of full-data OPD performance because its on-policy rollouts rapidly visit a surprisingly large fraction of the states reached by full-data OPD.
- Key result: one query reaches 71.5% state coverage; 16 semantically diverse queries reach 98.9% and match full-data training. The bottleneck is slow absorption of supervision rather than lack of data.
- Why it matters: strengthens the distinction between **semantic input diversity** and **trajectory/state coverage**. For research idea generation, a useful diversity objective may be coverage of distinct reasoning/idea states or graph regions, not simply diversity of prompts or surface-form ideas.
- Priority: **High**.

## Sequential Beats Joint: On the Interplay between On-Policy Distillation and RLVR
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04108
- Date: 2026-09-03
- Category: RLVR; On-Policy Distillation; Dense vs Sparse Supervision
- Core idea: OPD-then-RL consistently outperforms pure OPD, pure RLVR, and joint combinations of teacher supervision with RL advantage.
- Interpretation: OPD first expands coverage over teacher-supported solutions, then RL sharpens within that support; optimizing both signals simultaneously can cause gradient/signal interference.
- Why it matters: a warning for research-idea RL designs that simply add process reward / teacher critique / dense supervision into one GRPO objective. Staging coverage-building and reward optimization may be stronger than fusing every signal at once.
- Priority: **High**.

## Environment Evolution for Terminal Agents
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04128
- Date: 2026-09-03
- Category: Agentic RL; Environment Evolution; Curriculum; Harness
- Core idea: evolves environment difficulty off-policy and schedules evolved environments generation by generation so stronger policies continue receiving useful learning signals.
- Method: derives three environment-difficulty evolution directions from the multi-turn learning objective and implements them with a multi-agent harness.
- Reported result: simple long-horizon RL improves Qwen3.6-27B and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B by 14.4 and 18.0 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1.
- Why it matters: relevant to open-ended scientific ideation because fixed idea/task distributions can become too easy or stale. The analogous design would evolve scientific problems, evidence constraints, or critique environments as the ideation policy improves.
- Priority: **High adjacent**.

## Synthesis

The strongest new signal is that process-level Agentic RL is moving toward a three-part design:

`coverage / curriculum -> reliable localized credit -> reward sharpening`

and that each stage can fail if its signal is naively interpreted. In particular, raw GRPO advantage may be spurious, dynamic rubrics can supply credit when terminal verifiers are unavailable, and diversity should increasingly be measured as state/trajectory coverage rather than surface-form variation.
