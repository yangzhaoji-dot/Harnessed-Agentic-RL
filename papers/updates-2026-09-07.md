# Literature Updates — 2026-09-07

High-priority papers surfaced in the Monday 7 September 2026 arXiv listings and the most recent submissions. Focus: Harness × Agentic RL, scientific/research agents, credit assignment, persistent graph/skill structures, and diversity.

## 1. What Does Multi-Harness RL Learn? Credit Assignment and Portability in Coding Agents
- arXiv: 2609.04518
- Submitted: 2026-09-03
- Category: Harnessed Agentic RL; Credit Assignment; Generalization
- Model: Qwen3-8B, SFT warm start
- Harnesses: Aider, OpenHands, Qwen Code, SWE-agent; minimal held-out harness for portability evaluation
- RL: GRPO-style relative optimization, comparing within-harness grouping vs cross-harness grouping
- Benchmark: SWE-bench Verified
- Main result: evaluation harness dominates measured solve rate; cross-harness relative advantage encodes harness identity but provides essentially no additional held-out-harness portability over within-harness credit.
- Why important: a warning that reward/advantage in harnessed RL can learn configuration-specific adaptation rather than portable capability. For any future adaptive-harness or idea-graph RL method, grouping boundaries and held-out-harness evaluation become critical controls.

## 2. CoSkill: Joint Reinforcement Learning of Reasoning and Meta-Skill Agents for Hierarchical Skill Evolution
- arXiv: 2609.04865
- Submitted: 2026-09-04
- Category: Skill-Policy Co-Evolution; Multi-Agent RL; Hierarchical Skill Evolution
- Model/backbone: shared backbone for Reasoning Agent and Meta-Skill Agent; exact parameter scale to verify from full paper
- Harness/skills: hierarchical task skill + step skills; meta-skill workflow itself is learned rather than fixed
- RL: joint cooperative RL of Reasoning Agent and Meta-Skill Agent
- Benchmarks: ALFWorld, WebShop
- Results: 98.4% ALFWorld, 90.6% WebShop, reported +3.5/+6.2 pp over prior skill/RL baselines
- Code: https://github.com/jinyuan-cookie/CoSkill
- Why important: further narrows novelty for generic "policy + evolving skill/harness" claims. The stronger research question is now how to assign causal credit across heterogeneous harness components, preserve useful diversity, or determine when external structure should be retained/retired.

## 3. EVOHARNESSBENCH: Can Your Agents Keep Pace with an Evolving Harness?
- arXiv: 2609.04280
- Submitted: 2026-09-03
- Category: Benchmark; Harness Evolution; Continual Adaptation
- Harness axes: tools, skills, specialist agents
- Dataset: 17 multi-stage harness streams; 802 tasks; 520 tools; 42 skills; 62 agents
- Evaluation: deployment retention + self-evolving adaptation
- Core finding: simply expanding the harness can degrade previously solved tasks (harness-induced forgetting); retention and adaptation can conflict.
- Why important: provides a concrete benchmark for a problem central to our Harness × RL direction: non-stationarity in the harness itself. It also suggests that "more harness capability" is not monotonic and that harness evolution needs explicit retention/adaptation objectives.

## 4. On Epistemic Diversity in Large Language Models
- arXiv: 2609.04835
- Submitted: 2026-09-04
- Venue: COLM 2026
- Category: Diversity Evaluation; Open-Ended Generation
- Core concept: epistemic diversity = range of valid answers, explanations, and reasoning routes exposed by a model
- Finding: frontier LLMs often collapse large valid spaces onto a small canonical subset despite remaining accurate.
- Why important: useful conceptual/evaluation basis for diversity-preserving research idea generation. Idea diversity should be measured over valid hypotheses / explanations / reasoning routes, not just lexical diversity or token entropy.

## 5. Evidence Integration in Large Language Models
- arXiv: 2609.04290
- Submitted: 2026-09-03
- Category: Scientific Reasoning; Evidence Integration; Agent/RAG Reliability
- Scale: >10M trials; 12 LLMs; 8 domains including quantum mechanics, physics, genetics, molecular biology
- Finding: evidence influence is receiver-relative; models can integrate candidates even after internally verifying them as invalid. Identical evidence can help weaker models and hurt stronger models.
- Why important: directly relevant to scientific ideation agents that retrieve literature, receive critiques, or merge branches. A verifier saying "this evidence is invalid" does not guarantee the evidence stops causally influencing the final idea. Idea-graph systems may need explicit evidence arbitration / branch-weight updates rather than verbal self-verification alone.

## 6. What Matters in On-Policy Distillation? A Perspective on Data Efficiency and Data Selection
- arXiv: 2609.05198
- Submitted: 2026-09-04
- Category: OPD; Data Selection; Long-Horizon Reasoning
- Models: four models from 1.5B to 7B
- Result: 1-shot OPD can work; harder examples are often better; long CoT paths rather than token entropy explain much of the gain. Eight selected hard examples reportedly match a 17K-example baseline.
- Why important: reinforces the recent "coverage/state trajectory > raw data volume/entropy" theme. For research idea generation, selecting trajectories that induce long, structurally rich search/revision behavior may matter more than collecting many superficially diverse prompts.

## 7. Trace2Tower: Transition-Aware EigenTrace Induction of Multi-Level Skills for LLM Agents
- arXiv: 2609.05261
- Submitted: 2026-09-04
- Category: Persistent Experience Graph; Skill Induction; Agent Memory
- Structure: canonicalized events -> transition/outcome graph -> contrastive spectral decomposition -> hierarchy of action templates, routines, and task strategies
- Benchmarks: ALFWorld, WebShop
- Results: 87.31% ALFWorld success with 10.35 steps / 0.26 invalid actions; 50.67% WebShop exact success
- Why important: a useful structural analogue for an idea graph. Raw trajectories are converted into a persistent outcome-conditioned graph and then distilled into reusable hierarchy; this could inspire scientific idea graphs that retain transitions, successes/failures, and reusable reasoning motifs rather than only storing text nodes.

## 8. AutoLR: Automating the Path from Research to Launch Review in Industrial Recommender Systems
- arXiv: 2609.04871
- Submitted: 2026-09-04
- Category: Research Agent; Harness; Exploration-Exploitation
- System: multi-expert council + deterministic evidence-weighted exploration/exploitation selector + layered persistent knowledge
- Workflow: research literature -> candidate proposal -> implementation -> offline experiments -> evidence accumulation -> launch review
- Why important: one of the clearest recent examples of a long-running research harness allocating limited experimental budget across competing directions. Although industrial rather than scientific-discovery focused, its evidence-weighted branch selection is directly relevant to idea-graph search and experiment-budget allocation.

## Current synthesis

The strongest update from this batch is that three previously separate issues are converging:

1. **Credit is harness-dependent.** Cross-harness advantages may encode configuration identity rather than portable capability.
2. **Harness evolution creates its own non-stationarity.** Adding tools/skills/agents can cause forgetting, so harness growth is not monotonically beneficial.
3. **Diversity needs semantic/epistemic structure.** For open-ended research ideation, useful diversity should be defined over valid hypotheses/reasoning routes and preserved through branch/evidence arbitration, not approximated only by token entropy.

This makes a promising research abstraction: persistent idea/harness graph + explicit evidence/branch attribution + held-out-structure generalization + epistemic-diversity preservation.
